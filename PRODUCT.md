# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Relay is presented as a real AI help-desk product. Design for its pretend buyer and that buyer's customers:

- **Customers** who file a support request (`/new`) and then watch it on their ticket page (`/t/:ref`), often from a phone, sometimes after an email link.
- **Support operators** at a small SaaS company who watch the live ticket list, review AI output and recover failed tickets (`/ops`, signed in with an ops token).
- **Support leads evaluating the product** on the landing page (`/`): what it does, how it feels to their customers, what it costs.

The real audience is hiring engineers and recruiters for backend/platform roles. They should see a believable product. Architecture and tech details belong in the README, never on the site.

## Product Purpose

Relay reads each support request the moment it lands, sorts it by category and urgency, and drafts a reply. Anything it can't handle cleanly goes to a human. Success means a customer gets a useful answer within seconds, and the ops team only touches the tickets that need them.

## Positioning

Transparency: the customer **watches their ticket being understood and answered, live**. Received → Triaged → Drafted → Replied is streamed to their ticket page as it happens. Most help desks hide this behind a "we'll get back to you" email. Relay's identity is built on making the processing visible and trustworthy.

## Operating Context

- Public: submit a ticket (Turnstile-protected), track it by reference (`TICK-<13 digits>-<8 HEX>`), opt in to an email when the reply is ready (double opt-in, `/confirm/:token`). Recent tickets are remembered in the browser.
- Ops: KPI strip (24h tickets, AI success, latency, fallback rate), status tabs, sortable/paginated table, ticket drawer with the full event history, replay for failed / needs-attention tickets, live feed status, sign out.
- Ticket states the UI must express: received, triaging, drafting, completed (replied), failed, needs_attention, requeued. Failed/needs-attention show the customer one neutral message, never error details.
- Other routes: `/legal/:doc` (`privacy`, `terms`; `/privacy` and `/terms` redirect to it), 404.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Stack: Vite + React + TS + Tailwind v4 + shadcn/ui (Radix) in `web/`, served by the API from `web/dist`. TanStack Query; socket events only invalidate queries.
- Brand name and public copy live in `web/src/config/brand.ts`; landing and legal copy in `web/src/content/`.
- Dark only: there is one theme and no toggle. The Relay Full Demo (https://claude.ai/artifact/KgujCr5HHmouNMgx9bHwzo) is the single design authority; web/ copies its values exactly, and any departure forced by a hard gate is listed and approved. The Landing Demo and the light "paper" theme are retired.
- Hard quality gates: axe WCAG 2.1 AA on every route × key states, no horizontal scroll at 360px, keyboard + skip link, Lighthouse mobile ≥ 0.9 in performance / accessibility / best-practices / SEO on `/`, `/new`, `/t/:ref`, main JS chunk under 500 kB, fonts self-hosted, no `innerHTML`.
- Customer emails are HTML with inline styles (`src/services/notification-emails.ts`).
- Free-tier hosting: no paid services, assets, or fonts that need a licence fee.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: **Relay** (final). Tagline "AI help desk".
- The old violet/starfield look and `AI Ticket Pipeline — Design Concept.html` are retired. They're evidence of what the product is, not authority for how it looks.

## Evidence on Hand

- Real product behaviour: the live pipeline, fake-LLM mode for demos (`LLM_MODE=fake`), and the landing demo built from the real stepper/thread components.
- Pricing (Free / $49 Popular / Custom) is cosmetic; no checkout exists.
- There are **no** real customers, testimonials, logos, usage stats or benchmarks. Never invent them. Demo tickets must be labelled or obviously illustrative.
- README screenshots and the customer-flow GIF are generated by `bun run screenshots`.

## Product Principles

1. Show the work: progress is always visible, specific and live, never a spinner standing in for an explanation.
2. Calm when things fail: failure states are honest, neutral and give the customer a next step; details stay with ops.
3. Believable, not demonstrative: everything looks like a product a support lead could buy, not a tech demo.
4. Accessible by default: the AA and keyboard gates are product requirements, not polish.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA, full keyboard use, skip links, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected (the landing demo shows its final frame), live regions on the ticket thread.
